    Worksheet Generalizing Number Patterns
    

    Objectives

    • Given a list of numbers that changes by the same amount each time, I can describe the pattern in words, find numbers that come before or after a given value, and use a variable to write expressions for the numbers around any value in the list.
      🔗
    🔗
    For each number pattern below, describe the rule in your own words, find a missing value, and then use a variable to generalize the relationship.
    🔗

    1. Problem 1.

    Consider the following list of numbers:
    \begin{equation*} 3,\ 7,\ 11,\ 15,\ 19,\ 23,\ 27,\ \dots \end{equation*}
    🔗

    (a)

    Describe in your own words the pattern for getting the next number in the list.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Add \(4\) to a number to get the next number in the list.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Suppose the number \(103\) is somewhere in this list. What number comes exactly two spots before it? Show your work or explain how you know.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Going back one spot subtracts \(4\text{,}\) so going back two spots subtracts \(8\text{.}\) The number two spots before \(103\) is \(103 - 8 = 95\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Suppose a variable \(x\) represents some number in this list. Fill in the empty boxes below with expressions using \(x\) to represent the surrounding numbers in the pattern.
    🔗
    
    Four boxes in a row with an ellipsis on each end. From left to right, the boxes are labeled Previous, Current, Next, and Two spots after. The Current box contains \(x\text{,}\) and the other three boxes are empty.
    🔗
    Answer.
    The previous number is \(x - 4\text{,}\) the next number is \(x + 4\text{,}\) and the number two spots after is \(x + 8\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    2. Problem 2.

    Now consider this list of numbers, which follows a different pattern:
    \begin{equation*} 50,\ 43,\ 36,\ 29,\ 22,\ 15,\ 8,\ \dots \end{equation*}
    🔗

    (a)

    Describe in your own words the pattern for getting the next number.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Subtract \(7\) from a number to get the next number in the list.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Suppose the number \(-62\) appears much later in this list. What number comes exactly three spots before it? Show your work.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Each step forward subtracts \(7\text{,}\) so going back one spot adds \(7\text{.}\) Going back three spots adds \(3 \cdot 7 = 21\text{.}\) The number three spots before \(-62\) is \(-62 + 21 = -41\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Suppose \(x\) is a number in this list. Fill in the boxes with expressions representing the surrounding numbers.
    🔗
    
    Four boxes in a row with an ellipsis on each end. From left to right, the boxes are labeled Previous, Current, Next, and Two spots after. The Current box contains \(x\text{,}\) and the other three boxes are empty.
    🔗
    Answer.
    The previous number is \(x + 7\text{,}\) the next number is \(x - 7\text{,}\) and the number two spots after is \(x - 14\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    3. Problem 3.

    Consider this list that includes decimal values:
    \begin{equation*} 2.5,\ 4.0,\ 5.5,\ 7.0,\ 8.5,\ 10.0,\ 11.5,\ \dots \end{equation*}
    🔗

    (a)

    Describe in your own words the pattern for getting the next number.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Add \(1.5\) to a number to get the next number in the list.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Suppose the number \(31.0\) is in this list. What number comes exactly two spots after it? Show your work.
    🔗
    Answer.
    Going forward two spots adds \(1.5\) twice, or \(3\text{.}\) The number two spots after \(31.0\) is \(31.0 + 3 = 34.0\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    If \(x\) is a number in this list, fill in the boxes with expressions for the number before it and the two numbers after it.
    🔗
    
    Four boxes in a row with an ellipsis on each end. From left to right, the boxes are labeled Previous, Current, Next, and Two spots after. The Current box contains \(x\text{,}\) and the other three boxes are empty.
    🔗
    Answer.
    The previous number is \(x - 1.5\text{,}\) the next number is \(x + 1.5\text{,}\) and the number two spots after is \(x + 3\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    4. Problem 4: Build Your Own Pattern.

    (a)

    Make up your own list of numbers where you add or subtract the same amount every time. Choose a starting number and an amount that are different from Problems 1 through 3.
    🔗
    , , , , , , …
    🔗
    Answer.
    Answers will vary. One possibility is \(6, 15, 24, 33, 42, 51, \dots\text{,}\) which starts at \(6\) and adds \(9\) each time.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (b)

    Describe in your own words the pattern for getting the next number in your list.
    🔗
    Answer.
    For the example list, add \(9\) to a number to get the next number.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (c)

    Suppose \(x\) is a number in your list. Fill in the boxes with expressions representing the surrounding numbers.
    🔗
    
    Four boxes in a row with an ellipsis on each end. From left to right, the boxes are labeled Previous, Current, Next, and Two spots after. The Current box contains \(x\text{,}\) and the other three boxes are empty.
    🔗
    Answer.
    For the example list, the previous number is \(x - 9\text{,}\) the next number is \(x + 9\text{,}\) and the number two spots after is \(x + 18\text{.}\)
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    (d)

    Trade papers with a neighbor. Check that their expressions in the previous part match the rule they wrote for their list.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗
    Writing expressions in terms of \(x\) helps us see the structure of a pattern no matter where we are in the list.
